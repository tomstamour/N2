3. Detection logic (simple but effective)

You want a scoring model, not a binary rule.

Example scoring:
def squeeze_score(data):
    score = 0

    # --- SHORT PRESSURE ---
    if data.si_percent > 20:
        score += 2
    if data.days_to_cover > 3:
        score += 2

    # --- VOLUME EXPANSION ---
    if data.rel_volume > 3:
        score += 2
    elif data.rel_volume > 2:
        score += 1

    # --- PRICE ACTION ---
    if data.price_change_5m > 2:  # %
        score += 2
    if data.breakout:
        score += 2

    # --- FLOAT CONSTRAINT ---
    if data.float < 50_000_000:
        score += 1

    return score

How likely is it that the nasdaq short interest data published twice a month can estimate that a stock is likely to be in a short squeeze set up two weeks after the data was published?

Short answer: on its own, the twice-monthly short interest from Nasdaq is a weak predictor of a squeeze two weeks later—but it’s still useful as a background filter.

Let’s break that down more precisely.

1. The core problem: time lag

Short interest data has two layers of delay:

Settlement lag (T+2 style mechanics)
Publication lag (reported twice a month)

👉 By the time you see it, the data is often:

~5–10 trading days old
And you’re asking about 2 weeks later, so now you're potentially 15–25 days behind reality

That’s an eternity in short-selling terms.

2. What actually changes in those 2 weeks?

A lot—and this is why predictive power drops:

Shorts are dynamic
Funds can enter/exit quickly
Covering can happen in 1–3 sessions
New shorts can pile in
A stock with 20% SI can become 40% quickly
Or drop to 5% just as fast

👉 So the reported number becomes a stale snapshot of positioning

3. What does remain predictive

Even with the lag, short interest still carries structural information:

Reliable signals (persist over weeks):
Extremely high SI % (e.g. >30–40%)
High days to cover (>5–7)
Low float stocks

These don’t change instantly.

👉 This is why stocks like GameStop or heavily shorted small caps can stay “squeeze candidates” for weeks.

4. What is not predictive after 2 weeks
Exact timing of a squeeze ❌
Whether shorts have already covered ❌
Intraday pressure ❌

This is where most strategies fail.

5. Realistic predictive power (quant intuition)

Think of it this way:

Short interest alone (2-week old)
→ maybe slightly better than random for squeeze detection
Short interest + real-time triggers
→ becomes meaningfully predictive

In practical terms:

SI is a necessary condition
But not a sufficient trigger
6. How pros actually use it

They treat it like a pre-filter, not a signal:

Step 1 — Build a watchlist

Step 2 — Wait for fresh signals

This is the key:

Old positioning + new pressure = squeeze

MOre details about this, Short volume (daily, FINRA), is that free?

Yes — FINRA short volume data is free, and it’s actually one of the most useful (and misunderstood) datasets you can plug into a squeeze detector.

Let’s break it down properly so you can use it correctly.

1. Is FINRA short volume free?

Yes.
The FINRA publishes it publicly:

Daily downloadable files (TXT/CSV)
Available the same day around ~6pm ET

Free for non-commercial use

👉 You can literally download it every day and feed it into your algo.

2. What exactly is “short volume”?

This is where most people go wrong.

Definition:
It’s the number of shares sold short during a given day
Aggregated per stock

BUT:

👉 It is flow (activity), not position

3. Short volume vs short interest (critical distinction)
Metric	Meaning	Update frequency
Short Interest	Total open short positions	2x/month
Short Volume	Shares sold short that day	Daily

FINRA explicitly warns:

Short volume ≠ short interest

Why?

A short trade can be opened and closed the same day
So it shows up in volume…
But never appears in short interest
4. What data you actually get

From FINRA daily files:

For each stock:

short_volume
total_volume (FINRA-reported subset)
date

👉 You can compute:

Short Volume Ratio=
total volume
short volume
	​

5. Important limitation (this is big)

FINRA data:

Covers mainly off-exchange trades (dark pools, TRFs)
Does NOT include all exchange trades
Is not consolidated

👉 So:

It is incomplete, but still very useful as a signal

6. Why traders still use it

Because it gives something short interest does NOT:

👉 Timing

Short interest:

Tells you who is trapped

Short volume:

Tells you what shorts are doing right now
7. How to use it in a squeeze detector (practical edge)

Here’s where it becomes powerful.

Key signal:
Rising short volume + rising price

That means:

Shorts are still entering → but price is going up → they’re getting trapped

Example logic:
short_ratio = short_volume / total_volume

if short_ratio > 0.5 and price_up:
    signal += "potential trap building"
Even stronger signal:

Track multi-day behavior:

if short_ratio_today > short_ratio_yesterday and price_up:
    signal += "increasing short pressure"
8. The real “edge” use-case

Combine:

1. Old short interest (structure)
SI % > 20%
2. Recent short volume (flow)
Elevated or rising
3. Real-time trigger

👉 This combination is far more predictive than SI alone.

9. What NOT to do (very important)

Don’t assume:

“50% short volume = 50% of trades are new shorts” ❌
“High short volume = high short interest” ❌

Because:

Market makers generate short volume for liquidity
Positions can close same day
