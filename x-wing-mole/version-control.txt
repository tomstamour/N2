x-wing-mole — version control
================================================================================
IBKR (ibapi) trading trio. A trade-frequency surge detector (trade-mole) hands a
buy-signal to a yield-laddered trailing-stop trader (x-wing); a clerk runs BOTH
as an in-process "duo" on ONE shared, pre-warmed ibapi client from a pool.

Live chain (three separate processes):
    RTPR_connector3 ─tcp8900─► orchestrator5 ─tcp8765─► clerk-1.1 ─► Gateway
Current live scripts: clerk-1.1.py, trade-mole-2.2.py, x-wing-2.1.py.
clerk-1.2.py (trio, paper-verify pending) loads trade-mole-2.4.py (2.2 until
2026-09-17, then 2.3 for one day, then 2.4 on 2026-09-18),
T47-airspeeder-2.0.py and x-wing-3.0.py.
Run everything with the venv python (system python3 lacks ibapi).
Ports: 4001 = LIVE Gateway, 4002 = paper GW, 7497 = paper TWS.

Legacy X-wing-1.0.py and trade_mole_4.1.py have been DELETED (refactor complete);
only their successors remain on disk.


================================================================================
x-wing  —  yield-laddered trailing-stop trader (long side, one symbol)
================================================================================
Constant across versions:
  - On buy-signal: BUY LMT for floor(CAPITAL / entry) shares, then a protective
    SELL STP LMT that RATCHETS (only ever moves UP, holds when price falls). A
    synthetic trigger converts it to a SELL LMT at the held table limit to force
    the ETH-safe exit. Yield -> Trigger%/Limit% comes from the banded limits TSV.
  - Never uses reqGlobalCancel (would nuke other instances on the shared account)
    or reqTickByTickData (3-request cap).

  THREE FIXES.
  (A) _exit_repair_price() -- ONE source of truth for the repair price, and it
      honours WHICH trigger fired. _trigger_source == "hard_floor" (equivalently
      _exit_lmt_sent is None, which is what the control loop records at the
      synthetic trigger) means the exit must be marketable at the BID: the hard
      floor IS the loss cap, and a limit resting above the market caps nothing.
      Otherwise the held ladder limit. 3.2 reached for _lmt_floor unconditionally
      and that is defect 2 of the JUNS chain.
  (B) rearm_dead_exit(), called from the control loop's exiting branch BEFORE
      chase_exit_limit. When the resting exit reads Cancelled / ApiCancelled /

      re-places on a FRESH orderId.
      ⚠ A fresh id is the point. arm_or_replace_protective reuses
      self.protective_id by design -- same id means modify, which is what the
      trailing ladder wants -- so re-placing on a dead id draws 103 again. The id
      is cleared by the supervisor before the call, never inside it.
      Bounded exactly like every other order path here: EXIT_REARM_MAX_N = 5

      as dead -- re-placing over a working exit would double the sell side.
      Budget spent logs ERROR once, naming the position: nothing in the process
      can close it before the lifetime flatten, so that line is the operator's
      only warning. Resets on a new entry fill, with the ratchet state.
  (C) exit_wall_obs / exit_wall_ref / _note_exit_wall() -- the SELL-side mirror
      of entry_wall_obs. A 202 on the protective id carries the EXACT floor and
      IBKR's own reference; 3.2 parsed both inside the handler and threw them
      away, so every later repair was free to re-send a refused price. Now
      recorded FIRST, before the guards that return early (the reading is just as
      valuable on an attempt we decline to make), keeping the HIGHEST floor seen
      this run, and _exit_repair_price lifts over it with _wall_step_up.
  Plus: the shutdown FLATTEN now clears a dead protective id and prices through
  _exit_repair_price(force_bid=True), so it cannot repeat the 15:15 failure. And
  the 3.2 budget-spent log line claimed "the control loop will flatten" -- it did
  not and never could; the handover is now named instead of assumed.

  KILL SWITCH: --no-exit-rearm (EXIT_REARM_ENABLED=False) reverts (B) exactly.
  (A) and (C) are not switchable: a repair price that ignores the trigger, or a
  wall reading thrown away, are defects with no configuration to preserve.

  THE PROBLEM. Three trades on 2026-10-01 (MEDS, VEEA, HXHX, labelled _early_ in
  xwing_tables/) exited 1-9 s after the entry fill, at prices that have no
  corresponding trade on a TradingView chart. The whole exit path runs off the
  BID -- current_exit_ref() returns it, yield_pct is (bid-entry)/entry,
  high_water is the peak BID, aux = high_water*(1-Trigger%/100). TWO separate
  stops then exist:
      1. a REAL IBKR STP LMT resting at the Gateway. triggerMethod is never set,
         so IBKR evaluates it on the LAST TRADED price. This one was CORRECT and
         never fired.
      2. x-wing's OWN check, `if exit_ref <= aux:` in control_loop, once a
         second, on the BID. On fire it replaces the stop-limit with a plain
         SELL LMT, which has no trigger and sells straight into the book.
  (2) did the damage. A bid is an offer that can be WITHDRAWN with no shares
  changing hands, so the level it ejects at need never have traded -- hence no
  print on the chart. In 6 of 26 triggered exits it fired while the last trade
  AND the ask were still at/above the entry fill: MEDS (entry 3.73, ejected on
  bid 3.58 with last 3.73 / ask 3.75), VEEA, EDBL, IMCC, KIDZ (entry 2.83,
  ejected on bid 2.45 while last and ask were 2.90 -- a -13% loss on a stock
  that was UP), NAMI.
  Measured over 31 filled sessions 2026-09-28..10-01: median spread at fill
  4.23% (v7) / 2.34% (v8); 18 of 31 reached a positive yield at some point but
  only 3 ENDED positive. The losses are made on the EXIT, not the entry: v7 gave
  the gains back (HCTI +11.3%->-6.1%, ZBAO +21.5%->-3.4%) and v8 ejected before
  the move started. Winners took 8-119 s to reach their peak; v8 was exiting in
  1-9 s.

  ⚠ NOT the cause, and checked first: re-anchoring the yield on the bid at fill
  changes NOTHING. LimitsTable.lookup() floors at the first row, so a yield of
  -5.22% and one of 0.00% both select the `Yield 0` row (row_yield_threshold is
  0.0 in all three files), and aux comes from high_water, not from the anchor.
  The bid is the RIGHT price for accounting -- it is where a sell fills. It is
  the wrong price for the DECISION "is this falling?".

  THE CHANGES (all flag-gated; DRIVEN mode reads the module constants, so those
  defaults are the live configuration -- build_config does not carry them):
  - exit_decision() replaces the bare `if exit_ref <= aux:`. Three outcomes:
      hard_floor / hard_floor_bid  unconditional loss cap, no confirmation
      bid+last                     bid at/below aux AND a real trade agrees
      deferred                     bid crossed aux, no trade did -> HOLD
    EXIT_REQUIRE_TRADE_CONFIRM=True. A silent or >30 s stale tape (
    EXIT_CONFIRM_MAX_LAST_AGE_SEC) gives no trade signal, so the bid stands
    alone there and the row says so (bid_only_stale_last) -- without that escape
    the position would be left protected only by the floor.
  - the spread band. EXIT_TRIGGER_SPREAD_MULT=1.5:
    eff_trigger% = max(table_Trigger%, 1.5 * MEDIAN spread% over 10 s), capped
    at EXIT_SPREAD_MAX_PCT=20. The table value is now a FLOOR. The MEDIAN
    matters: HXHX quoted 0.43/0.44 (2.3%) for one tick while its 10 s median was
    4.9%, and the median is the honest measure of a name's noise scale.
  - ⚠ the RATCHET had to be split. _aux_floor/_lmt_floor stay the EFFECTIVE held
    levels (on_order_error's fallback and the `exiting` branch read them), and
    new _aux_table_floor/_lmt_table_floor carry the pure profit ratchet. aux =
    min(ratcheted_table_aux, hw*(1-eff_trigger%/100)). Without the split the
    ratchet pushes the stop UP into the noise band, which IS the MEDS failure.
  - the hard floor. HARD_FLOOR_PCT=5.0, HARD_FLOOR_ON_LAST=True. ⚠ On the last
    print this caps the TRADED decline at 5%, NOT the realised loss -- the sale
    happens at the bid, a spread lower (replay: it fires at a bid of -8.57% on
    NUR, -12.51% on QCLS). A plain 5% BID floor is not the answer either: VEEA's
    bid fell 5.6% on noise with `last` unmoved, so it would fire on that and
    undo the confirmation. So the bid clause is spread-widened too:
    bid <= entry*(1-(5%+spread%)/100). A name with a 12% spread (LRHC) simply
    cannot be exited for a 5% loss; this makes that explicit.
  - chase_exit_limit(). LESL 2026-09-30: the bid gapped 0.2320->0.1815 straight
    THROUGH aux 0.2085 and the SELL LMT at 0.2074 rested UNFILLED for 860+ s
    while the position bled. Now re-priced DOWN to the live bid. ⚠ Bounded
    exactly as the 2026-08-17 IVF storm requires: EXIT_LMT_CHASE_MAX_N=5,

2026-07-28  BUGFIX (clerk-1.1.py AND clerk-1.2.py) — deferred sessions were
            killed by a sentiment gate that did not apply to them
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Symptom: BKYI (PR 02:59 ET, deferred to a 04:00 collection start) surged at
  04:00:02 and trade-mole recorded ZERO data points / wrote no output file.

  Cause: the "Sentiment BAD" handler resolved the trigger regime with
  conditions.at(datetime.now()) — i.e. the instant the BAD ARRIVED (02:59 ->
  DEFAULT -> finbert_gate=on -> tear down). But the session was deferred to
  collect at 04:00, which resolves to WINDOW 04:00-06:59 -> finbert_gate=off,
  where sentiment has NO authority and the surge is the sole trigger. finish()
  then cancelled the pending defer timer, so reqMktData was never issued and the
  session was gone 61 min before the surge.

  This contradicted the design already stated elsewhere in the file:
  on_buy_signal resolves finbert_gate at the SURGE instant, and
  _begin_collection anchors the watch lifetime on COLLECTION START, explicitly
  noting "04:00 ET for deferred overnight duos, not trigger arrival". The BAD
  handler was the one place that missed the same consideration. For a live-market
  session arrival ~= surge, so it never showed there.

  Hit rate on the affected path was 100%: of 5 deferred sessions in 8 days of
  clerk logs, 4 died this way (PMI 07-21, CRMT 07-21, MSGM 07-22 on clerk-1.1;
  BKYI 07-28 on clerk-1.2). EDHL 07-26 had no BAD, survived, and collected
  normally at 04:00 — the control case.

  Fix: new DuoSession/TrioSession.conditions_instant() returns the scheduled
  collection start while the session is still deferred (_defer_target set and
  _watch_timer not yet armed) and `now` once collection has begun; the BAD
  handler resolves against it. The existing gate-off branch already did the right
  thing — it was simply never reached. _defer_target is left untouched so the
  crash-replay record and arm reply are unaffected.

  BEHAVIOUR CHANGE (intended): an overnight BAD-sentiment PR now survives to
  04:00 and may trade on surge alone, which is what finbert_gate=off means for
  that window. Intraday behaviour is unchanged (BAD still tears the session down).

  Also: Orchestrator5.2 now logs the three gate inputs against their thresholds
  on the BAD path ("[Gate] SYM BAD: positive=... indecisive=... combo=..."), so a
  rejection is diagnosable from the log without joining it to the TSV by hand.
