# Entry-order failure modes — findings (last updated 2026-07-27)

Two DISTINCT failure modes make a marketable BUY LMT entry come back with no
fill. Part A is now CONFIRMED. Part B remains unexplained.

================================================================================
PART A — IBKR PRICE CAP (error 2161)  ** CONFIRMED 2026-07-27, PFSA **
================================================================================

## What it is
IBKR does not reject an over-aggressive marketable limit; it ACCEPTS it, silently
lowers the working price to its allowed band, reports back "Submitted", and sends
error 2161 as a warning. The order is alive the whole time — just resting at a
price we did not choose. Verbatim (clerk.log 2026-07-27 06:00:01.154):

  code=2161: BUY 2 PFSA NASDAQ.SCM @ 2.15  for U18283054  In accordance with our
  regulatory obligations as a broker, we will initially cap (or limit) the price
  of your Limit Order to 1.76 or a more aggressive price still within your
  specified limit price.  If your order is not immediately executable, our
  systems may, depending on market conditions, cap your order to a limit price
  that is no more than the allowed amount away from the reference price at that
  time. If this happens, you will not receive a fill. [...]

This CONFIRMS candidate cause #1 in the old version of this file. No Gateway API
message log was needed — 2161 arrives on the normal error() callback.
NOTE: 2161 predates x-wing-2.1 (seen 07-20 and 07-22), so the Price Management
Algo is NOT the cause. It is IBKR's standard broker price cap, applied either way.

## The PFSA incident (2026-07-27), clerk.log:508-557
  06:00:00.708  duo armed. iti_baseline=2442.90 (~41 min between trades — a dead
                name). lastClose=1.71.
  06:00:00.891  Rule-F PRETRADE surge, 0.9 s into the entire session. Cumulative
                volume 1,700 sh. last=1.81 stamped 06:00:00. Quote 1.65 x 2.05.
  06:00:00.898  ENTRY: BUY LMT 2 PFSA @ 2.1500  (ask 2.05 x 1.05), orderId 26
  06:00:01.154  ERROR 2161 -> capped to 1.76
  06:00:01.159  "Submitted"  — live, but working at 1.76, ~14% under the offer
  06:00:00-:06  quote frozen at 1.65 x 2.05
  06:00:07-:10  ask runs 2.15 -> 2.16 -> 2.44 (now above our own 2.15 limit)
  06:00:10.910  ENTRY_FILL_TIMEOUT_SEC=10 fires -> we cancel the resting BUY
  06:00:11.008  Cancelled, no fill -> run aborted, logged as "entry_inactive"

THE CANCEL WAS OURS. IBKR never rejected and never deactivated the order.

Trades really did print around 2.25 during and after the run (verified on
TradingView). They were simply unreachable: a buy limit working at 1.76 fills
only against offers at or below 1.76. We were a passive bid ~20% under the market.

cap/ask sits in 0.975-1.030 for ELEVEN of twelve; cap/close scatters 0.96-1.13.
=> the reference is normally the OFFER, and the cap lands essentially on it.

PFSA is the lone outlier, and the reason is TIMING, not a bad quote: it fired
0.9 s into the session, when IBKR had no tape to price a gap from. Its reference
was still the prior close 1.71 (x1.03 = 1.7613 -> 1.76) or the 1.81 print
(x0.97 = 1.7557 -> 1.76) — both round to the observed cap exactly. Every other
capped fire happened deep into pre-market with a live tape, so its reference had
already caught up to the offer.

*** Rule F (pretrade staleness) is the only fire path that can trigger this
early, so it is structurally exposed to the stale-reference cap. ***

## The cap DOES release — and releasing has always cost money
IBKR re-caps toward the moving reference, so a capped order can eventually work
its way up to our full limit. Outcomes of all six capped orders that filled:

  sym    latency   filled at                        outcome
  TOPS   0.5 s     0.8799  (at the cap)             -12.38%
  PRFX   0.5 s     1.1400  (below the cap)           -3.51%
  KAPA   1.8 s     0.3727  (below the cap)           +8.99%  (peak +19.45%)
  BAOS   7.2 s     2.9100  FULL LIMIT, cap released -13.40%
  NIVF   9.3 s     1.3000  FULL LIMIT, cap released -11.54%
  GWH    23.0 s    0.9464  FULL LIMIT, cap released  -9.35%

All THREE cap-release fills lost 9-13%. The only winner filled in 1.8 s BELOW
its cap, i.e. a normal immediate fill. This reproduces, on the capped population
specifically, the finding that drove the 30->10 s timeout cut (x-wing-2.1.py:157):
"of 27 entries that took >1s, only 3 made money; every fill past 15s lost bar one".

## CONCLUSION: the cap is protective, not a bug to work around
PFSA not filling was the system working. The cap held us out of a 10-second
spike top on a name that trades every 41 minutes.

REJECTED — an ask-chasing entry (re-price the resting limit as the ask moves,
ceiling'd by MAX_CAP_ENTRY_PERCENT). Considered 2026-07-27, rejected because:
  1. The cap is set relative to IBKR's reference, not to our limit. Bidding
     2.56 instead of 2.15 still gets capped to ~1.76. Chasing cannot beat a cap.
  2. IBKR already chases for us, bounded above by our limit — that is exactly
     what the "FULL LIMIT, cap released" rows above are.
  3. It manufactures the late chased-up fills that lost money 3 times out of 3.

REJECTED — extending ENTRY_FILL_TIMEOUT_SEC past 10 s to let the cap ratchet.
This is what would have "fixed" PFSA, and it is precisely what bought BAOS
(-13.40%), NIVF (-11.54%) and GWH (-9.35%).

Caveat: n=3 in the cap-release group. Small. Revisit if it grows and reverses.

## Known gap in our instrumentation
2161 is not in ENTRY_REJECT_CODES (x-wing-2.1.py:148), so x-wing's
on_order_error (:955) drops it. Only the clerk's catch-all error() handler
(clerk-1.1.py:340-347) logs it. Consequence: x-wing's own log, its price table,
and the clerk's "finishing duo (xwing:entry_inactive)" line record a capped run
with the SAME label as a genuine IBKR deactivation (Part B) — two completely
different problems, indistinguishable in every historical grep.

To identify a capped run today you must cross-reference clerk.log for a "code=2161"
line on the same reqId. Diagnostic signature: cap/ask well below 1.0 means a stale
early-session reference; cap/ask ~= 1.0 is a routine cap that usually fills fast.

Open idea (NOT implemented): abort immediately when cap/ask is far below 1.0, so
the run frees its pooled clerk client instead of resting 10 s at an unfillable
price. On the evidence above this forgoes nothing.

PART B — SILENT "Inactive" ENTRY  ** STILL UNEXPLAINED **
================================================================================

Distinct from Part A: here IBKR immediately marks the entry "Inactive" with
filled=0 and NO error() callback and no reason code. No 2161, no 202, nothing.
x-wing's ENTRY_DEAD_STATUSES guard (x-wing-2.1.py:154, handled in
on_order_status :928-941) aborts the run ("entry_inactive"). The order was never
live — this is IBKR deactivating it, not our code cancelling it.

## What NCRA newly rules out
1. The Price Management Algo does NOT prevent Part B. ENTRY_USE_PRICE_MGMT_ALGO
   is True (x-wing-2.1.py:129 -> :699 -> :815) and orderId 40's placeOrder wire
   dump carries it. x-wing-2.1 has been live since 2026-07-23, so NCRA is the
   FIRST Part-B case with the algo attached — and it died anyway. The 2.1
   mitigation addresses Part A only; do not expect it to help here.
2. Limit aggressiveness is NOT the driver. The three 4.00%-pad cases were the
   basis for the "too aggressive" theory; NCRA used a 0.40% pad (~0.6% over the
   ask) and was deactivated faster than two of them.
   PROVENANCE of that 0.40%: it is not an x-wing decision, it is a local
   working-tree edit. As of 2026-07-29 x-wing-2.1.py has UNCOMMITTED changes
   MAX_LIMIT_ENTRY_PERCENT_PRICE   5.0 -> 0.4   (:106)
   STALE_QUOTE_ENTRY_PERCENT_PRICE 6.0 -> 3.0   (:118)
   (git HEAD still holds 5.0 / 6.0). The edit predates today's 08:00 run, so the
   0.40% pad was genuinely live for NCRA. Anyone re-reading the table above
   should not conclude x-wing narrowed the pad on its own — and should note that
   every future run stays on the tight pad until those constants are committed or
   reverted, which changes fill behaviour well beyond this investigation.

## Status of the candidate causes
1. Pre-market price cap on a marketable limit  -> SUPERSEDED. That mechanism is
   real but it is Part A, and it announces itself with 2161 and leaves the order
   Submitted. It does not produce a silent Inactive.
2. Venue session had only just opened at the top-of-hour boundary and briefly
   rejected the order -> STILL OPEN, and now clearly the leading candidate. All
   FIVE occurrences sit within seconds of a session-open boundary, and #1/#2
   above have removed the competing explanations.

The previous text here said to enable "Create API message log file". IT IS
ALREADY ENABLED and has been for weeks; the logs were simply being looked for in
the wrong directory. The live Gateway is IBC-managed and started with
  --tws-settings-path=/home/tom/Jts/settings-live
(see `ps`: /home/tom/IBC/live/scripts/ibcstart.sh 1030 -g), so API logs live in

  ~/Jts/settings-live/pehghglnegcfnbchbdpcghnkjbmkglkilclcnofo/

NOT in ~/Jts/gdopcnffpajbfdbiofimaemhbdphkopgaflihmln/ (that is the other
profile). One file PER CLIENT ID:

  api.<clientId>.<yyyymmdd>.<hhmmss>.ibgzenc

To map a run to its file: take the clerk.log line "client NNNN connected" for the
session and match clientId + that connect timestamp. For NCRA that is
  api.1018.20260729.035921.ibgzenc     (2655 bytes)
because clerk.log has "03:59:21.458 client 1018 connected; nextValidId=40".
The sibling files for OFAL / CLIK / AIHS are still on disk under the same scheme.

These files are AES-encrypted (header "IBGZENC\0", writerVersion 3, 16-byte IV,
length-prefixed keyId "aaa", then length-prefixed AES/CBC/PKCS5Padding chunks
over a gzip stream — see twslaunch/jutils/ibgzenc/{k,l,a,b} in
Jts/ibgateway/1030/jars/twslaunch-1030.jar; note k.a is the 8-byte magic, NOT the
key). There is no jts.ini flag to write them in plaintext.

## Meanwhile: we were also discarding reasons ourselves (fixed 2026-07-29)
Independently of the encrypted logs, our own callbacks threw away the only fields
IBKR does send on a deactivation:
  - orderStatus receives whyHeld and never forwarded it to on_order_status
  - openOrder logged at DEBUG and kept only orderState.status, dropping
    warningText and completedStatus
Fixed in x-wing-2.1.py, clerk-1.1.py and clerk-1.2.py: whyHeld is now threaded
into on_order_status(..., why_held="") and appears in the "Entry order N Inactive
with no fill (whyHeld=...)" warning, in the "BUY status" line, and in a NEW
trailing why_held column on the x-wing price table. openOrder is now INFO and
logs status/completedStatus/warningText. The `event` value is unchanged
("entry_inactive") so analyze_trade_mole/analyze_firstrade_outcomes.py's
NO_FILL_EVENTS set-match still works, and the new column is appended last so
older TSVs still parse. If the NEXT occurrence carries a whyHeld or warningText,
it will self-diagnose with no IBKR round-trip.

Current handling (unchanged, no auto-retry)
================================================================================
x-wing-2.1.py treats Inactive/Cancelled/ApiCancelled on an unfilled entry as
terminal:
  - ENTRY_DEAD_STATUSES   (:154)
  - orderStatus abort     (:928-941)  -> entry_failed="entry_inactive"
  - ENTRY_REJECT_CODES    (:148) + on_order_error (:965) for coded rejects
  - ENTRY_FILL_TIMEOUT_SEC=10 backstop (:1041-1055), cancel + confirm
2161 is deliberately NOT in ENTRY_REJECT_CODES: a capped order is alive and may
still fill, so treating it as a reject would be wrong.
