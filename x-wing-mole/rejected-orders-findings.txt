# Entry-order failure modes — findings (last updated 2026-08-13)

Two DISTINCT failure modes make a marketable BUY LMT entry come back with no
fill. Part A is now CONFIRMED. Part B remains unexplained.

================================================================================
PART A — IBKR PRICE CAP (error 2161)  ** CONFIRMED 2026-07-27, PFSA **
================================================================================

## What it is
IBKR does not reject an over-aggressive marketable limit; it ACCEPTS it, silently
lowers the working price to its allowed band, reports back "Submitted", and sends
error 2161 as a warning. The order is alive the whole time — just resting at a
price we did not choose. Verbatim (clerk.log 2026-07-27 06:00:01.154):

  code=2161: BUY 2 PFSA NASDAQ.SCM @ 2.15  for U18283054  In accordance with our
  regulatory obligations as a broker, we will initially cap (or limit) the price
  of your Limit Order to 1.76 or a more aggressive price still within your
  specified limit price.  If your order is not immediately executable, our
  systems may, depending on market conditions, cap your order to a limit price
  that is no more than the allowed amount away from the reference price at that
  time. If this happens, you will not receive a fill. [...]

This CONFIRMS candidate cause #1 in the old version of this file. No Gateway API
message log was needed — 2161 arrives on the normal error() callback.
NOTE: 2161 predates x-wing-2.1 (seen 07-20 and 07-22), so the Price Management
Algo is NOT the cause. It is IBKR's standard broker price cap, applied either way.

                name). lastClose=1.71.

THE CANCEL WAS OURS. IBKR never rejected and never deactivated the order.

Trades really did print around 2.25 during and after the run (verified on

only against offers at or below 1.76. We were a passive bid ~20% under the market.

=> the reference is normally the OFFER, and the cap lands essentially on it.

PFSA is the lone outlier, and the reason is TIMING, not a bad quote: it fired
0.9 s into the session, when IBKR had no tape to price a gap from. Its reference
was still the prior close 1.71 (x1.03 = 1.7613 -> 1.76) or the 1.81 print
(x0.97 = 1.7557 -> 1.76) — both round to the observed cap exactly. Every other
capped fire happened deep into pre-market with a live tape, so its reference had
already caught up to the offer.

*** Rule F (pretrade staleness) is the only fire path that can trigger this
early, so it is structurally exposed to the stale-reference cap. ***

## The cap DOES release — and releasing has always cost money
IBKR re-caps toward the moving reference, so a capped order can eventually work
its way up to our full limit. Outcomes of all six capped orders that filled:

  sym    latency   filled at                        outcome
  TOPS   0.5 s     0.8799  (at the cap)             -12.38%
  PRFX   0.5 s     1.1400  (below the cap)           -3.51%
  KAPA   1.8 s     0.3727  (below the cap)           +8.99%  (peak +19.45%)
  BAOS   7.2 s     2.9100  FULL LIMIT, cap released -13.40%
  NIVF   9.3 s     1.3000  FULL LIMIT, cap released -11.54%
  GWH    23.0 s    0.9464  FULL LIMIT, cap released  -9.35%

All THREE cap-release fills lost 9-13%. The only winner filled in 1.8 s BELOW
its cap, i.e. a normal immediate fill. This reproduces, on the capped population
specifically, the finding that drove the 30->10 s timeout cut (x-wing-2.1.py:157):
"of 27 entries that took >1s, only 3 made money; every fill past 15s lost bar one".

## CONCLUSION: the cap is protective, not a bug to work around
PFSA not filling was the system working. The cap held us out of a 10-second
spike top on a name that trades every 41 minutes.

REJECTED — an ask-chasing entry (re-price the resting limit as the ask moves,
ceiling'd by MAX_CAP_ENTRY_PERCENT). Considered 2026-07-27, rejected because:
  1. The cap is set relative to IBKR's reference, not to our limit. Bidding
     2.56 instead of 2.15 still gets capped to ~1.76. Chasing cannot beat a cap.
  2. IBKR already chases for us, bounded above by our limit — that is exactly
     what the "FULL LIMIT, cap released" rows above are.
  3. It manufactures the late chased-up fills that lost money 3 times out of 3.

  ** SUPERSEDED 2026-08-12 by x-wing-3.0.py — but only points 1 and 3, and only
     because 3.0 is a different mechanism from the one rejected here. **
  All three points above stand as written about an UP-ONLY, un-time-boxed chase.
  What this paragraph never considered is that point 2 is the PROBLEM, not the
  consolation: IBKR chasing for us, bounded above by a limit we priced once
  minutes ago, IS the stale-ceiling ratchet. Measured over all 248 filled
  entries, every one of the 20 that took >10 s paid 100% of the pad. The static
  limit is already an uncontrolled chase; it is simply run by IBKR, on IBKR's
  schedule, up to a number we chose before the tape existed.
  3.0 therefore keeps a limit ~2.5% above the LIVE ask in BOTH directions:
    - re: point 1 — agreed, and 3.0 does not try to beat the cap. It stays near
      the offer, which is where the band is anyway (cap/ask 0.985-1.030 on 11 of
      the 12 pairable caps), and it now RECORDS the cap: 2161's price is parsed
      out of the warning and openOrder is routed through so the working limit is
      visible. That closes the "Known gap in our instrumentation" below.
    - re: point 3 — the UP follow is time-boxed to 3 s (ENTRY_TRACK_UP_WINDOW_SEC)
      and ceiling'd at trigger_ref +6%, so it cannot reach the 7-23 s window the
      three losers filled in. The DOWN ratchet is what is new and is not
      time-boxed: it is the only thing that prevents a limit from becoming the
      stale ceiling that produced BAOS/NIVF/GWH.
  The caveat below ("n=3 in the cap-release group. Small.") still applies, and
  now cuts both ways: the case FOR the down ratchet rests on the same 3 fills.
  Operator decision 2026-08-11: ceiling +6%, not the price-neutral +2.9%, i.e.
  worst-case entry price is deliberately allowed above 2.1's. Watch it.

REJECTED — extending ENTRY_FILL_TIMEOUT_SEC past 10 s to let the cap ratchet.
This is what would have "fixed" PFSA, and it is precisely what bought BAOS
(-13.40%), NIVF (-11.54%) and GWH (-9.35%).

Caveat: n=3 in the cap-release group. Small. Revisit if it grows and reverses.

## MGRX vs REBN, 2026-08-13 — the cap meets the 3 s UP-window
** The matched pair that motivated ENTRY_CAP_RESCUE_ENABLED (x-wing-3.0). **

Both ran the SAME day on the SAME clerk-1.2 (client pool, x-wing-3.0), both drew
a 2161 that parked them UNDER the offer, and they ended opposite ways. The only
difference is WHEN the ask legged up relative to ENTRY_TRACK_UP_WINDOW_SEC=3.0.

  REBN 07:30:00.685  entry 1.7000 (ask 1.6400 x1.025), ceiling 1.7500
       07:30:01.260  2161 -> working 1.6300  (cap/ask 0.994, under the offer)
       07:30:02.460  ask legs 1.64 -> 1.72 at t=1.775 s, INSIDE the window
                     -> CHASE_CEIL 1.7000 -> 1.7500
       07:30:02.599  FILLED 2 sh @ 1.7200, 139 ms later

  MGRX 16:05:01.450  entry 0.5915 (ask 0.5770 x1.025), ceiling 0.6117
       16:05:01.750  2161 -> working 0.5764  (cap/ask 0.999, under the offer)
       16:05:01-05.9 ask FLAT at 0.5770 -> tracker correctly idle, n=0
       16:05:05.989  ask legs 0.5770 -> 0.5970 at t=4.539 s, OUTSIDE the window
                     -> target computed as 0.6117 (marketable!), then refused:
                        _track_up_frozen = True, PERMANENTLY
       16:05:06-11   ask runs 0.5970 -> 0.6000 -> 0.6100, we never bid again
       16:05:11.651  ENTRY_FILL_TIMEOUT_SEC -> our cancel -> "entry_inactive"
       later         MGRX traded 0.7347 by 16:47 (+27.3% off the trigger)

Three things this pair establishes:

1. REBN FILLED AT 1.7200 — ABOVE its own original 1.70 limit. Raising our limit
   is therefore exactly what produces the fill while capped, and point 1 of the
   2026-07-27 rejection above ("chasing cannot beat a cap") needs its scope
   stated precisely: chasing cannot move IBKR's REFERENCE, but our limit is the
   ENVELOPE IBKR is allowed to work inside ("...or a more aggressive price still
   within your specified limit price"). A limit left at 1.70 caps the release at
   1.70. That is a real, measured lever, and it is the one MGRX never pulled.

2. The window check is LAZY (x-wing-3.0.py, inside `if target > working +
   delta_floor`). It does not fire at t=3.0 s; it fires on the FIRST up-move the
   tracker actually WANTS after t=3.0 s. On a flat-then-jump tape that is the
   very move that would have filled — the window does not expire quietly, it
   expires ONTO the rescue. Any future reasoning about the box must use this
   shape, not "3 seconds of chasing then stop".

3. The "don't chase after 3 s" evidence (84.6% of fills land <=3 s; BAOS 7.2 s
   -13.40%, NIVF 9.3 s -11.54%, GWH 23.0 s -9.35%) is about a WORKING order. A
   capped order is not working: IBKR holds it below the offer, so it can neither
   fill nor chase while the clock runs out on it. MGRX spent its whole window
   inert (n=0 modifies) and was then punished as though it had been chasing.

FIX (2026-08-13, x-wing-3.0.py): ENTRY_CAP_RESCUE_ENABLED — ONE post-window UP
move, armed only while entry_capped_to is set AND the price IBKR is working sits
below the live ask (entry_lmt_working when openOrder was forwarded, else the 2161
cap itself; MGRX had no openOrder, so the fallback is load-bearing). Blocked
outright by a 202. Still bounded by the +6% ceiling, the 20-modify budget, the
0.25 s throttle and the 10 s timeout. Replaying MGRX's recorded ask series
through the patched controller yields exactly one CAP_RESCUE at the leg-up,
0.5915 -> 0.6117 against a 0.5970 ask (+2.46% marketable), covering the 0.6100
ask peak for the rest of the pending window. REBN replays UNCHANGED — it chased
inside the window, so the rescue never arms. Runs with no 2161 are untouched.

⚠ This does NOT reopen "extend the timeout to let the cap ratchet", still
REJECTED above: the rescue is one bounded re-price inside the same 10 s, not
more time. And it does not contradict PFSA — PFSA's cap/ask was 0.859, so a
rescue there would still have left it ~14% under the offer and unfillable.
The pattern this converts is cap/ask ~= 1.0 (11 of 12 caps) plus a late leg up.

⚠ Also fixed here: a 202 arriving as the ACK OF OUR OWN CANCEL was being logged
as "hit IBKR's aggressive-limit wall (202)". MGRX logged that 96 ms after its own
timeout-cancel. Harmless as a log line; not harmless once 202 blocks the rescue.
The wall branch is now gated on `not _entry_abort_pending`.

## Known gap in our instrumentation  ** CLOSED in x-wing-3.0, 2026-08-12 **
2161 is not in ENTRY_REJECT_CODES (x-wing-2.1.py:148), so x-wing's
on_order_error (:955) drops it. Only the clerk's catch-all error() handler
(clerk-1.1.py:340-347) logs it. Consequence: x-wing's own log, its price table,
and the clerk's "finishing duo (xwing:entry_inactive)" line record a capped run
with the SAME label as a genuine IBKR deactivation (Part B) — two completely
different problems, indistinguishable in every historical grep.

FIXED in x-wing-3.0.py (still open for anything running 2.1, i.e. clerk-1.1):
  - on_order_error now handles 2161 explicitly, parses IBKR's capped price out
    of the warning text (_parse_capped_price) and writes an ENTRY_CAPPED row.
    Still deliberately NOT in ENTRY_REJECT_CODES — a capped order is alive.
    Logged ONCE per run: a tracked entry can draw a fresh ~1 kB 2161 on every
    re-price.
  - Both clerks now forward openOrder to ctl.on_open_order (getattr-guarded, so
    clerk-1.1 keeps loading 2.1 unchanged). openOrder carries the only copy of
    the price IBKR is ACTUALLY working — orderStatus never reports one — so a
    cap that lowers the working price below our limit is now visible live,
    not reconstructed from clerk.log afterwards.
  - New price-table columns: entry_order_id, entry_lmt, entry_status, track_n,
    capped_to (appended after why_held, so 2.1-era parsers still read the file).
    `capped_to` non-empty is the discriminator this section asks for; a run with
    entry_status=Inactive and capped_to empty is Part B.

To identify a capped run today you must cross-reference clerk.log for a "code=2161"
line on the same reqId. Diagnostic signature: cap/ask well below 1.0 means a stale
early-session reference; cap/ask ~= 1.0 is a routine cap that usually fills fast.

Open idea (NOT implemented): abort immediately when cap/ask is far below 1.0, so
the run frees its pooled clerk client instead of resting 10 s at an unfillable
price. On the evidence above this forgoes nothing.

## How to tell the two apart in practice (added 2026-08-11)
The collision above is not theoretical — it bit this month. As of 2026-08-11,
  grep -l entry_inactive xwing_tables/*2026-08*.tsv
returns 10 files, of which only 6 are Part B. The other 4 are OUR OWN
ENTRY_FILL_TIMEOUT_SEC cancels, IBKR status "Cancelled", not "Inactive":
  ORIO 2026-08-06 07:39:01.8 -> 07:39:11.9  (+10.2 s)
  UZX  2026-08-11 05:05:36.4 -> 05:05:46.5  (+10.1 s, clerk code=202)
  CYAB 2026-08-11 09:00:13.7 -> 09:00:23.8  (+10.1 s)
  GLE  2026-08-11 09:04:11.2 -> 09:04:21.2  (+10.1 s)

Two reliable discriminators, neither needing clerk.log:
  1. The x-wing log string. Part B says "Inactive with no fill"; a timeout cancel
     says "Cancelled with no fill". The IBKR status differs (Inactive vs
     Cancelled) even though ENTRY_DEAD_STATUSES maps both to "entry_inactive".
  2. Time from ENTRY to death. Part B is sub-second (47-809 ms observed); a
     timeout cancel lands at ~10 s by construction.
So: sweep xwing-logs/ for "Inactive with no fill", NOT xwing_tables/ for
"entry_inactive". The 2026-08-11 Part-B table was built that way.

Second trap, specific to the T47 era: a trio writes ONE x-wing log but ONE TABLE
PER RUN, so table names and log names do not map 1:1. AIHS 2026-08-11 has tables
..._06-58-00.tsv and ..._06-58-19.tsv but a single log ..._06-58-00.log holding
both deactivations. Naive per-table lookup misfiles run 2. Count events by
grepping the LOGS, then attribute to runs via the "ENTRY: ... orderId=N" line.

## Why 3 of those 4 cancels were unfillable from the start (FIXED in 3.0)
ORIO aside, UZX / CYAB / GLE all rested at a price MAX_CAP_ENTRY_PERCENT=30.0
(x-wing-2.1.py:120) had clamped below the market, so the 10 s timeout was a
formality. GLE is the clearest and most expensive: T47 run 1 filled 11 @ 0.4387
and exited stop_filled at 0.6693 (+52.6%) — and the run-2 re-entry was then priced
at 0.5034 against a 0.6693 bid, 25% under, because the clamp is anchored to
last_close and the name had already doubled off it. T47 relaunches BY DESIGN only
after price recovers to the frozen SP1, so on any name that ran far from
last_close the re-entry cannot fill. Same mechanism produced the passive AIHS
run 2 in the Part B table below. Recorded here only so the pattern is on paper;
fixing it is deliberately not part of this update.

** FIXED 2026-08-12 in x-wing-3.0.py. ** MAX_CAP_ENTRY_PERCENT now defaults to
None; the entry ceiling is trigger_ref * (1 + ENTRY_TRACK_MAX_PCT/100), anchored
on the price we actually decided to buy at rather than on a close the name has
already left. Swept over all 317 entries, the last_close clamp priced SEVEN of
them below the market — GLE -24.8%, UZX -19.8%, NUWE -43.2%, BYAH -41.7%,
ICCM -70.4%, OBAI -17.8%, BNRG -12.1% — i.e. it was not a GLE quirk. Verified on
the GLE shape in analyze_trade_mole/verify_xwing3_tracking.py: with last_close
1.50 and a 2.10 bid, 2.1 clamps run 2 to 1.95 (unfillable) and 3.0 prices it at
2.18. The --max-cap-entry-percent flag still exists for standalone experiments.

Current handling (unchanged, no auto-retry)

x-wing-2.1.py treats Inactive/Cancelled/ApiCancelled on an unfilled entry as
terminal:
  - ENTRY_DEAD_STATUSES   (:154)
  - orderStatus abort     (:928-941)  -> entry_failed="entry_inactive"
  - ENTRY_REJECT_CODES    (:148) + on_order_error (:965) for coded rejects
  - ENTRY_FILL_TIMEOUT_SEC=10 backstop (:1041-1055), cancel + confirm
2161 is deliberately NOT in ENTRY_REJECT_CODES: a capped order is alive and may
still fill, so treating it as a reject would be wrong.

x-wing-3.0.py keeps all of the above and adds ONE deliberate exception, because
it now sends modifies and a REJECTED MODIFICATION can surface as a transient
"Cancelled" on an order that is in fact still live. A Cancelled/ApiCancelled
landing within ENTRY_TRACK_STATUS_GRACE_SEC=0.75 s of a re-price is DEFERRED,
and taken only if no live status follows. **This is scoped to Cancelled: a
Part-B "Inactive" still aborts immediately**, so the 12-event/2790-run series
above stays directly comparable across the 2.1->3.0 boundary. Do not widen it.
Still no auto-retry anywhere: an entry is never re-placed under a new orderId
(T47's late-fill guard keys on ctl.active_buy_id and assumes one entry id per
run), and a modify-class error stops tracking rather than retrying.
