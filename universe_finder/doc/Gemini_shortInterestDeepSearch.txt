Quantitative Frameworks for Short Squeeze Prediction in Small-Cap Equities Using Open-Access Market and Securities Lending DataShort squeezes represent highly non-linear market events where a rapid appreciation in a security's price forces short sellers to cover their positions by purchasing shares, creating a self-reinforcing upward price spiral. In small-cap equities, these dynamics are exceptionally pronounced due to structural factors such as limited float, high retail sentiment volatility, and severe frictions in the securities lending market. To systematically identify these opportunities, market participants must synthesize multiple open-access data feeds to model the cost of shorting, the regulatory settlement timeline, float mechanics, and derivatives market imbalances. This report outlines a comprehensive, quantitative, no-code framework for predicting short squeezes in small-cap stocks using a standard Interactive Brokers (IBKR) Level 1 data bundle alongside free public regulatory databases.Securities Lending Infrastructure and Interactive Brokers Level 1 DataTo analyze the supply-demand friction of the securities lending market, practitioners utilize three primary metrics: the short borrow fee rate, shortable shares, and lending utilization. In the Interactive Brokers Trader Workstation (TWS) and Client Portal, these variables are broadcast in real-time through customized quote monitors and the Stock Loan Borrow (SLB) search utility. By adding these columns to a watchlist, market participants obtain a baseline view of the instant friction facing short sellers.Short Borrow Fee Rate and Carrying Cost FormulationsThe short borrow fee rate represents the annualized interest rate charged daily to borrow shares, which is determined by the supply of lendable shares from broker inventory or fully-paid lending programs relative to short demand. While blue-chip equities typically carry negligible borrow fees of approximately $0.25\%$, hard-to-borrow small-cap stocks frequently experience borrow fees exceeding $50\%$ to $100\%$ annualized. This high borrow fee acts as a continuous cash drain on short positions, establishing a high break-even threshold for short sellers.The carrying cost of a short position over a specific holding period is calculated mathematically as follows:$$C_{\text{daily}, t} = \frac{P_{\text{close}, t} \times N_{\text{shares}} \times f_t}{360}$$Where:$C_{\text{daily}, t}$ is the daily borrow cost charged on day $t$.$P_{\text{close}, t}$ is the closing price of the security on day $t$.$N_{\text{shares}}$ is the total number of shares shorted.$f_t$ is the annualized indicative borrow fee rate expressed as a decimal.A rapidly rising $f_t$ indicates a contracting borrow supply or accelerating demand, which often precedes short covering as the holding cost begins to outweigh the expected downward price movement.Lending Utilization and Share ScarcityUtilization represents the ratio of shares currently lent to the total shares available for borrowing within the securities lending pool, expressed as a percentage:$$\text{Utilization}_t = \left( \frac{\text{Shares Lent}_t}{\text{Shares Available for Borrow}_t} \right) \times 100$$A high utilization rate ($>90\%$) indicates that the stock is in high demand for shorting relative to its lendable inventory, making it highly susceptible to borrow supply shocks. When utilization reaches $100\%$, initiating new short positions becomes physically impossible due to a lack of locates. This limits further downward pressure and leaves existing short sellers vulnerable to rapid price reversals, as there are no new short sellers available to absorb upward buying pressure.Open-Access Historical Lending DataWhile real-time SLB data is available in IBKR TWS, historical backtesting requires historical data. Market participants can access this historical data without cost using web scrapers targeting the public API endpoints of iBorrowDesk, an open-access platform that captures and archives daily borrow rates, rebate rates, and available share snapshots directly from IBKR's lending pool. The historical data is retrieved via HTTP requests to the platform’s unstructured endpoints:$$\text{Endpoint Structure: } \texttt{https://iborrowdesk.com/api/ticker/\{TICKER\}}$$This public endpoint yields a historical daily record containing the following fields: fee, available, and timestamp, allowing quant analysts to map changes in fee rates and inventory levels alongside price charts to identify historical precedents of short covering rallies.Daily Transactional Flow Analysis and Short Volume MetricsA critical distinction must be maintained between official short interest and daily short volume. Short interest is the cumulative stock of open, uncovered short positions, which is reported bi-monthly by exchanges. Daily short volume, conversely, measures the flow of transactions executed as short sales on a specific trade date.Short Volume Ratio CalculationsThe Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA) publishes daily short sale volume files for all off-exchange trades executed through its Trade Reporting Facilities (TRF), Alternative Display Facilities (ADF), and Over-the-Counter Reporting Facilities (ORF). This data is free for non-commercial use via the FINRA Data Portal or Query API.The Daily Short Volume Ratio ($SVR_t$) is calculated as:$$SVR_t = \frac{V_{\text{short}, t}}{V_{\text{total}, t}}$$Where:$V_{\text{short}, t}$ is the daily aggregated volume of trades marked as short sales reported to FINRA.$V_{\text{total}, t}$ is the daily aggregated total volume of all transactions executed and publicly disseminated on the same reporting venues.To construct a comprehensive Aggregate Short Volume Ratio, analysts can combine daily short volume statistics across multiple venues, including the Cboe Exchange and Nasdaq PSX/BX, which publish daily short transaction records on their respective trader portals.$$\text{Aggregate Short Volume}_t = V_{\text{FINRA}, t} + V_{\text{Cboe}, t} + V_{\text{Nasdaq PSX/BX}, t}$$Microstructure Interpretation of Rising SVRA consistently high or rising $SVR_t$ ($>50\%$) over multiple trading sessions indicates intensive daily short-selling execution. When a small-cap stock experiences a high $SVR_t$ while the underlying price remains flat or moves upward, it signals strong buying absorption. In this scenario, market makers and directional short sellers are aggressively selling short to suppress price momentum, creating a crowded short trade that is highly vulnerable to a positive news catalyst or volume spike.Capital Structure Audits and Free Float MechanicsThe velocity and magnitude of a short squeeze are fundamentally governed by the scarcity of the tradable share supply. In small-cap stocks, evaluating the total shares outstanding is insufficient; one must model the true liquid float.Calculating Free Float and Insider ConcentrationUsing SEC EDGAR, market participants can extract the necessary capital structure variables from periodic reports (Forms 10-K and 10-Q) and proxy statements (Form DEF 14A). The Free Float represents the portion of shares outstanding that are actively trading in the public market.The mathematical formula to estimate Free Float is:$$\text{Free Float}_t = \text{TSO}_t - \text{Shares}_{\text{insider}, t} - \text{Shares}_{\text{restricted}, t}$$Where:$\text{TSO}_t$ is the Total Shares Outstanding, obtained from the cover page of the most recent Form 10-Q or 10-K.$\text{Shares}_{\text{insider}, t}$ is the sum of all shares held by corporate officers, directors, and beneficial owners holding $>10\%$ of the voting stock, disclosed in the latest DEF 14A or beneficial ownership filings (Form 3, 4, and 5).$\text{Shares}_{\text{restricted}, t}$ represents unregistered or locked-up shares not eligible for public resale under Rule 144.Days to Cover (DTC)Days to Cover (or the Short Interest Ratio) measures the time required for all short sellers to buy back their shorted shares under average trading volume conditions:$$\text{DTC}_t = \frac{\text{Short Interest}_t}{\text{ADV}_{n, t}}$$Where:$\text{Short Interest}_t$ is the bi-monthly reported short interest position.$\text{ADV}_{n, t}$ is the $n$-day Average Daily Volume (typically calculated over a 10-day or 30-day window).A DTC value greater than $5.0$ indicates that a short seller cannot exit positions quickly without causing a substantial price impact, multiplying the squeeze potential.Float Liquidity Space (Unshorted Float)The Float Liquidity Space measures the absolute quantity of liquid shares that remain unshorted:$$\Phi_{\text{unshorted}, t} = \text{Free Float}_t - \text{Short Interest}_t$$In highly shorted small-cap stocks, $\Phi_{\text{unshorted}, t}$ can approach zero, indicating that short sellers have borrowed and sold nearly the entire tradable supply. In extreme scenarios, due to re-hypothecation (where borrowed shares are sold to a buyer who subsequently lends them out again), short interest can exceed $100\%$ of the free float, creating an absolute mathematical deficit of shares available for delivery during a coordinated covering event.Monitoring Corporate Dilution RiskShort sellers often targeted small-cap stocks because of their weak balance sheets and high risk of dilutive financing. To mitigate short squeeze risk, short sellers actively seek out stocks that can issue new shares, which increases the float and provides them with a cheap cover. Market participants can track this dilution risk by searching SEC EDGAR for specific filing types:Form S-3 (Shelf Registration): Allows an issuer to register a large volume of securities in advance and sell them incrementally to the public over a multi-year period.Form 424B5 (Prospectus Supplement): Filed when an issuer executes an offering under an active S-3 shelf registration, such as an At-The-Market (ATM) equity program.Warrants and Convertible Securities: Disclosed in the notes to financial statements, these instruments allow holders to convert debt or preferred stock into common shares at a predetermined exercise price, introducing sudden equity dilution.If a small-cap stock has an active ATM program or significant outstanding warrants near the current trading price, any upward price momentum may be met with immediate corporate dilution, satisfying the short covering demand and neutralizing the squeeze potential. Conversely, if an issuer has no active S-3 registration or is bound by strict lock-up agreements, the float remains structurally capped, maximizing short squeeze risk.Settlement Failures and Regulatory Forced Buy-In CyclesFailures-to-Deliver (FTDs) occur when a broker-dealer fails to deliver the required shares to settle a transaction within the standard settlement cycle. Under SEC Regulation SHO, FTDs are critical indicators of potential naked short-selling activity and represent a regulatory source of forced buying volume.SEC Fails-to-Deliver Data StructureThe SEC publishes consolidated FTD data files twice per month on its official Markets Data portal. These text files represent the net cumulative balance of all outstanding fails recorded in the National Securities Clearing Corporation's (NSCC) Continuous Net Settlement (CNS) system across all clearing members.The data release schedule is structured as follows:First Half of Month: Data spanning trading days from the 1st to the 15th is published at the end of the current month.Second Half of Month: Data spanning from the 16th to the end of the month is published around the 15th of the following month.The raw file layout contains structured parameters parsed as pipe-delimited values (|):$$\text{Format: } \texttt{Settlement Date | CUSIP | Ticker | FTD Quantity | Price | Issuer Name}$$Regulation SHO Threshold Security List CriteriaTo identify small-cap equities experiencing chronic settlement failures, analysts monitor the daily Regulation SHO Threshold Security List published by self-regulatory organizations (SROs) such as NASDAQ, NYSE, and FINRA. A security is placed on this threshold list if it meets the following criteria for five consecutive settlement days:Volume Threshold: The aggregate FTD balance at a registered clearing agency (NSCC) is equal to or greater than $10,000$ shares.Outstanding Shares Threshold: The aggregate FTD balance represents at least $0.5\%$ of the issuer’s Total Shares Outstanding (TSO).SRO Inclusion: The security is officially listed on an SRO's threshold register.For non-SEC reporting issuers, FINRA Rule 4320 dictates that the security must meet the $10,000$-share FTD minimum, and the aggregate fail position must be valued at $\$50,000$ or more based on the reported last sale price.Mandatory Close-Out Dynamics under Rule 204Under Reg SHO Rule 204, clearing firms are subject to mandatory close-out requirements to resolve open fail-to-deliver positions. The close-out timelines are determined by the nature of the transaction that generated the fail:Short Sale Fails (T+2 / S+1): If the FTD resulted from a short sale, the clearing firm must close out the position by purchasing shares of like kind and quantity in the open market no later than the beginning of regular trading hours on settlement day plus one ($S+1$).Long Sale / Market Maker Fails (T+4 / S+3): If the FTD resulted from a long sale or bona fide market-making activity, the clearing firm has until the beginning of trading hours on $S+3$ to purchase or borrow the necessary shares.If a threshold security’s FTDs persist for $13$ consecutive settlement days (or $35$ settlement days for specific historical locates or rule-restricted offerings), the broker-dealer is legally prohibited from executing further short sales in that security unless the security is first borrowed. This restriction, combined with mandatory close-out buying, creates price-inelastic purchasing volume in the open market, frequently triggering a short squeeze in low-liquidity small-cap stocks.Derivatives Imbalances and Options-Driven Gamma SqueezesThe derivatives market frequently acts as an primary accelerant for short squeezes in small-cap stocks. By monitoring options volume, open interest, and implied volatility structures via IBKR Level 1 option chains (OPRA market data), quantitative analysts can track speculative retail inflows and market maker hedging dynamics.Option Sentiment via the Put-Call Ratio (PCR)The Put-Call Ratio is a standard metric used to gauge speculative option sentiment. It is calculated using two methods:$$\text{PCR}_{\text{volume}, t} = \frac{V_{\text{put}, t}}{V_{\text{call}, t}}$$$$\text{PCR}_{\text{OI}, t} = \frac{\text{OI}_{\text{put}, t}}{\text{OI}_{\text{call}, t}}$$Where:$V_{\text{put}, t}$ and $V_{\text{call}, t}$ represent the daily trading volume of put and call options, respectively.$\text{OI}_{\text{put}, t}$ and $\text{OI}_{\text{call}, t}$ represent the total outstanding open interest of put and call options, respectively.While a PCR below $1.0$ indicates call-option dominance (bullish sentiment), extremely high PCR values ($>1.5$) represent a crowded bearish trade. For individual small-caps, a sudden shift where the PCR plunges below $0.5$ indicates aggressive speculative call buying, which can serve as an early warning sign of an options-driven squeeze.Implied Volatility (IV) Skew and SlopeImplied Volatility (IV) skew measures the difference in implied volatility between out-of-the-money (OTM) puts and calls. In a normal equity market, OTM puts trade at a premium to OTM calls due to downside hedging demand (negative skew).The option slope can be formulated as:$$\text{IV Slope}_t = \text{IV}_{\text{OTM Put}, t} - \text{IV}_{\text{OTM Call}, t}$$When a short squeeze is imminent, intense buying of OTM call options causes the relative implied volatility of calls to surge, flipping the skew positive (or flattening the IV slope). A precipitous drop in the IV slope indicates that market participants are bidding up call options to position for extreme upward volatility, making the stock highly attractive for momentum traders.Market Maker Gamma Hedging LoopWhen retail investors buy out-of-the-money call options, institutional market makers act as the liquidity providers by writing (selling) those calls. To maintain a risk-neutral profile, the market maker must hedge their short call position by purchasing shares of the underlying stock.The total delta-hedging share requirement ($\Delta_{\text{hedge}}$) is defined as:$$\Delta_{\text{hedge}, t} = N_{\text{calls}} \times \delta_t \times 100$$Where:$N_{\text{calls}}$ is the number of long call option contracts outstanding.$\delta_t$ is the dynamic rate of change of the option's theoretical value relative to the underlying stock price ($\frac{\partial V}{\partial P}$).As the underlying stock price rises toward the strike price of the call options, the delta ($\delta_t$) of those options increases non-linearly. This forces market makers to buy more shares of the underlying stock to remain delta-neutral. This positive feedback loop—where rising stock prices force market makers to buy shares, which further drives up the stock price and increases the delta of the options—is known as a "gamma squeeze." In small-cap stocks with thin order books, this mechanical buying can easily overwhelm the available float, triggering an explosive short squeeze.Integrated Quantitative Screeners and Analytical ComparisonTo implement these findings, market participants can aggregate the various feeds into a structured screening system.Quantitative MetricAnalytical Target / Operational FormulationPrimary Open-Access Data SourceCritical Squeeze ThresholdShort Borrow Fee Rate$f_t$ (Annualized daily borrow cost)IBKR TWS Watchlist / iBorrowDesk$>30\%$ annualizedLending Utilization$\frac{\text{Shares Lent}}{\text{Available Pool}} \times 100$[cite: 7]IBKR TWS / Orbisa Analytics$>90\%$ utilizationShort Volume Ratio (SVR)$\frac{V_{\text{short}}}{V_{\text{total}}}$ (Off-exchange flow)FINRA Daily Short Volume Files$>50\%$ combined with upward price actionFails-to-Deliver (FTD)Continuous Net Settlement Net OutstandingSEC Markets Data FTD FilesInclusion on SRO Threshold List for $>5$ daysDays to Cover (DTC)$\frac{\text{Short Interest}}{\text{Average Daily Volume}}$[cite: 25, 32]Mid/End-Month SRO Disseminations$>5.0$ trading daysLiquid Float Availability$\text{TSO} - Rather than treating any single indicator in isolation, market participants can operationalize this framework using a sequential analysis:Conduct a Float and Capital Structure Audit: Access the latest Form 10-Q or 10-K via SEC EDGAR to compute the true liquid float. Ensure that the outstanding unshorted float space is structurally constrained. Ensure the company has no active At-The-Market (ATM) programs or pending warrant offerings that could act as a cheap source of shares for short cover.Monitor Securities Lending and Carrying Costs: Using the IBKR Level 1 watchlist data, track daily shifts in borrow fee rates and utilization. A borrow fee rate rising past $30\%$ combined with utilization exceeding $90\%$ demonstrates that short sellers are facing high daily carrying costs and a lack of available shares to borrow.Screen for Off-Exchange Buying Absorption: Download the daily FINRA Short Sale Volume Files. Look for situations where the daily Short Volume Ratio exceeds $50\%$ over multiple sessions while the underlying stock price remains stable or moves upward. This combination indicates that short-selling pressure is being fully absorbed by institutional or retail buyers.Track Regulatory Forced Buy-In Cycles: Cross-reference the SRO daily databases for active listings on the Regulation SHO Threshold Security List. If a small-cap stock is listed on this threshold register and approaches the 13-consecutive-settlement-day limit, the mandatory close-out requirements under Rule 204 will force clearing firms to purchase shares in the open market, regardless of price.Evaluate Options Imbalances and Delta Hedging Loops: Use the IBKR Options Lattice and OptionTrader to monitor intraday options activity. A drop in the Put-Call Ratio below $0.5$, combined with a flattening implied volatility skew, indicates aggressive speculative call buying. This options imbalance forces market makers to buy underlying shares to maintain their delta-neutral hedges, triggering a gamma squeeze that accelerates the upward momentum.By combining these open-access metrics, analysts can identify when a heavily shorted small-cap stock is technically primed for a short squeeze. The convergence of these factors creates a highly asymmetric setup, where a positive fundamental catalyst or a sudden volume spike can trigger a rapid price appreciation as short sellers scramble to cover their positions.
